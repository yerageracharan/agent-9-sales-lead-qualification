A service request built around the property.

A possible future process, told through one illustrative customer scenario.

Scene 1

A guest is arriving. The rental unit has no cooling.

The property manager needs help with a specific unit and a time-sensitive situation. The office needs to know the property, who is affected and how access will work before deciding the next step.

What changesThe request begins with the property context, not a vague note saying “AC problem.”
Scene 2

The important details travel with the request.

The property and unit, manager contact, guest arrival window, cooling impact and access notes sit together. The office can understand why the timing matters before deciding how to respond.

What changesFewer calls to reconstruct the situation and confirm who can open the property.
Scene 3

The office coordinates access and service.

A staff member confirms access with the property manager and checks the service window with the team. The request shows those two actions separately so the job does not get treated as ready before both are resolved.

What changesAccess and service coordination become visible handoffs.
Scene 4

The property manager receives a useful update.

Once the team has confirmed the plan, the manager can receive a clear message describing what has been arranged and when they will hear back. The communication is prepared for staff review.

What changesThe manager knows the request was received and what happens next.
Scene 5

The office can see service activity by property.

Open requests can be reviewed by building, unit and impact. A repeat problem or unresolved access question is easier to spot when the service history remains attached to the property.

The intended outcomeProperty managers get a more organised service experience, and the team gets a cleaner handoff.

Three kinds of work. One shared picture.

A possible future process, told through one illustrative customer scenario.

Scene 1

Three requests arrive before the day gets going.

A homeowner asks about replacing a system. A commercial site reports a cooling issue. A builder wants to discuss a new project. Their needs are different, but the office has one shared view of the work.

What changesThe requests become visible work, not three separate reminders.
Scene 2

The request arrives with its context.

The office can see the work type, service area, what the customer needs and which details still need to be confirmed. The next step is chosen around the job, not forced into one generic checklist.

What changesThe team starts with enough context to make a useful decision.
Scene 3

The next person knows what they own.

A commercial request may need a site visit. A construction enquiry may need scope clarification. A residential replacement may need more information before an estimate can be prepared. Each handoff is explicit.

What changesThe work has an owner and a next action, not just a status.
Scene 4

An estimate sent still has a next step.

Once the team sends an estimate, the request moves into a decision stage. A follow-up date gives the office a reason to return to the conversation instead of relying on memory.

What changesOpen estimates remain visible until there is an outcome.
Scene 5

The owner sees where attention is needed.

The office view brings new requests, visits to coordinate and estimates awaiting decisions into one place. It does not replace dispatch or judgement. It makes the next move easier to see.

The intended outcomeA clearer operating picture across residential, commercial and construction work.

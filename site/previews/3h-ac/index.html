Follow the agreement, not just the next appointment.

A possible future process, told through one illustrative customer scenario.

Scene 1

A customer starts a maintenance agreement.

The agreement is recorded against the property and the covered HVAC system. Its two included visits become milestones that can be tracked separately.

What changesThe office can see what was promised and which system the visits belong to.
Scene 2

Both visits are visible from day one.

The first visit is expected around six months into the agreement, with the second about six months later. Each window can be adjusted to match the actual agreement and the customer's availability.

What changesCompleting the first visit does not make the annual agreement look complete.
Scene 3

When visit one is due, the right work is ready.

The office sees the visit approaching and starts scheduling. The technician receives the checklist that applies to the first service visit, based on the company's maintenance plan.

What changesThe appointment and the work included in that visit stay connected.
Scene 4

The second visit stays on the horizon.

After visit one is completed, the agreement advances to its second service window. If that visit is not scheduled or needs to be moved, the office sees it as upcoming, due or overdue.

What changesThe agreement is only fulfilled when both visits have been recorded.
Scene 5

Renewal begins with a clear service history.

As the plan approaches expiry, the office can see whether both visits were fulfilled and prepare a renewal conversation. The customer decides whether to continue.

The intended outcomeA complete view of the agreement: visits due, work completed and renewal timing.
